    Why EpisteMapper

    We help researchers see a field whole: what it predicted, how each test was designed, and what every design took for granted.

    An epistemic map (King, Goldfarb & Simcoe, 2021) is the correspondence between the assumptions a field has made and the evidence it obtained under each. EpisteMapper reads the core papers with you, lines up every prediction with the design of every test, shows where the evidence agrees, disagrees and has never looked, and turns the open cells into a multiverse that one researcher or many analyst teams can run.

    The ten steps
    What the tool does

    The AI reads and proposes, with a quote and a page for everything. You confirm, judge and decide; nothing enters the map until you do.

    Map every test

    State your inquiry and the studies you know; seal what you suspect the field assumes. The tool bounds the field, reads the core papers, and lines up every prediction with the design of every test on nine dimensions.

    Steps 1 to 5

    Judge what it assumes

    The tool unearths what the designs rest on and how sensitive each result is. You judge each assumption reasonable, questionable or violated; colleagues judge blind; the tool argues the other side, with a verdict that follows its argument. Then you decide.

    Step 6

    Decide what to test next

    A multiverse on existing data, run alone or allocated to analyst teams through the map; a replication of one study with one decision varied, planned in Empirical Navigator; both; or a new design. The result comes back into the map.

    What is an epistemic map?

    A map of a field at the level of its tests.

    Which predictions recur, which assumption sets were used to test them, where results disagree, and which assumptions no study has yet varied. The researcher is the cartographer, not the claims-maker: the map stays neutral, and the judgments live in its legend.
