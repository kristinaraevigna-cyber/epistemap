Coverage Map

An instrument for meta-research · empirical focus

Which predictions does a field rest on, and how have they been tested?

Coverage Map reads a field's core empirical studies, extracts the predictions they set out to test (X → Y) and the research design each one used, and lines them up: which predictions recur, which design decisions differ across studies, and where the results disagree. That is the raw material for a multiverse, and for choosing the next test. The AI proposes with a quote and page; you confirm every item.

Your fields

    Corpus

    Bound the field with OpenAlex or a Web of Science / Scopus export: seminal, recent and rising studies; reviews flagged. You decide which are core.

    2

    Studies

    Upload a PDF. The model extracts the predictions the paper set out to test, the design used for each, the result, and the analytical choices, with quotes and pages.

    3

    Predictions

    Confirm each prediction (X → Y at the construct level), merge the same prediction across studies, and mark the core ones.

    4

    Design map

    For each core prediction, the range of designs used across studies (data, sample, period, measures, estimator, controls, form, identification) and the result under each.

    5

    Choice space

    Compare the analytical choices the AI found with a published reference map (Berchicci & King, 2022 is built in).

    6

    Export

    Core predictions, the design decisions that matter most, a hand-off into Replication Navigator, and JSON and CSV for a multiverse.

    Papers are read once by the model and are not stored. Fields are saved on the server so a team can work on them together. Grounded in 322 verified evidence cards from the replication-methods literature. Built to feed Replication Navigator, which plans the test.
